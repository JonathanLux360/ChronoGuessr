**Alternatives in each year:** 1934 (the Italy World Cup final), 1971 (Swiss women's vote), 2008 (the Sichuan earthquake and the Lehman collapse) and 1935 (Braddock's title fight and Persia becoming Iran).

**Which Roosevelt law (1934).** I used the Gold Reserve Act, signed on 30 January 1934, because it is the law that ended gold as American money. The order forcing Americans to hand in their gold coins was an executive order in April 1933, not a law, and a June 1933 resolution cancelled the gold clauses in contracts. If you meant one of those, the clue belongs in 1933 and I can move it.

**The Saar vote (1935).** This is the plebiscite of 13 January 1935, in which about nine in ten voted to rejoin Germany. The region had been run by the League of Nations since the Treaty of Versailles. I first drafted the later referendum of 1955, which rejected a European plan, and moved it to 1935 as you asked. A player who knows the vote was in January will place it easily, but the clue itself carries no date.

**Zimbabwe (2008).** The government stopped publishing reliable figures at the peak, so the figure in the context line is an estimate by the economist Steve Hanke, which says prices doubled about every 25 hours. The clue says prices double "almost every day", and the context says "by one economist's estimate".

**From my own knowledge, not looked up (check first):** the Gold Reserve Act's price of about twenty dollars rising to thirty-five an ounce (the earlier price was $20.67); Nixon's August 1971 broadcast and the wage and price freeze; the 79.6 billion per cent figure and the 100 billion dollar note; and the Saar details: about nine in ten voting to rejoin Germany, the League of Nations running the region since 1920, and the Nazis using the result for propaganda.
