# Content review: batch 15 (the 1920s, 1930s and 1940s gaps)

**12 years**, 96 clues: the ten missing years of the 1930s and 1940s (1931, 1933, 1934, 1935, 1939, 1940, 1941, 1942, 1943, 1944) and the two missing years of the 1920s (1921 and 1925). I aimed for events that most players would recognise, and avoided the single most obvious event of a year where it would give the year away. Where a category has several clues, the first is the main clue and the rest are alternatives (the game draws one at random). Every clue is AI-drafted and **unverified** (`"verified": false`), so the game never shows it until you check it. A year goes live when every category has a verified clue. For each clue, open the source and check:

1. The event happened **in that year** (not across a span).
2. The text and the context line are accurate and neutral.
3. The clue does not give the year away on its own.
4. The source link works and supports the date.

**What was checked.** The dates come from the Wikipedia year pages and the linked articles I could open. Most Politics and Society items were confirmed only on the year page, and parts of some pages (the middle of several war years) were read only as summaries. Many context lines are from my own knowledge. A few clues come from my own knowledge only, because the year pages did not list them: the Capone verdict (1931), the Dambusters raid (1943), the Heinkel jet flight (1939), the Joliot-Curie discovery (1934), Florey's mouse test (1940), the Oscars and Hitchcock and Gershwin dates, and the V-weapon first use (1944).

**Date conflicts between a year page and its article (the clue uses only the year):**

- 1925 Baird: his first greyscale picture was in October 1925, but the first public demonstration of true television was in January 1926, so the clue does not say "first television".
- 1934 Donald Duck: the film was shown first in May, with its official release in June.
- 1935 Monopoly: the year page says sales began in February, but the article says November.
- 1939 Hewlett-Packard: the year page says 1 January, but the article says 2 July.
- 1940 helicopter: 13 May or 26 May.
- 1942 A-4 rocket: "first human-made object in space" is a claim made afterwards, using one definition of space.
- 1943 Colossus: the prototype was tested on 8 December 1943 and working at Bletchley Park in January 1944.
- 1944: the V-2 reached space in a test on 20 June, but the clue is about its first use against cities in September.
- 1931 cyclotron: the first working machine ran on 2 January 1931, but the idea dates from 1929 to 1930.
- 1931 China floods and 1943 Bengal famine: the death tolls are estimates and vary widely.

**Trimmed for obscurity.** 28 clues that most players could not place were dropped at your request, such as the cyclotron, the radar test, the Heinkel jet, the Aqua-Lung and the Bombay explosion. Kept on purpose, although flagged: the Bretton Woods conference (1944), the Vought-Sikorsky helicopter (1940), Goebbels's total war speech (1943) and the Four Freedoms speech (1941).

**Added to years already in the pool** (these are in the table at the end): Martin Luther King's "I Have a Dream" speech (1963) and To Kill a Mockingbird (1960). The founding of the People's Republic of China was already the main Politics clue for 1949 ("Mao Zedong proclaims the People's Republic of China from the Gate of Heavenly Peace in Beijing"), so nothing was added there.
