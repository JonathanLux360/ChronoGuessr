# Content review: batch 10 (three more ancient years)

**3 new years** from before 1 CE, built around a technology clue first (the first or decisive use of a technology, not only its invention). Every clue is AI-drafted and **unverified** (`"verified": false`), so the game never shows it until you check it. A year goes live when every category has a verified clue. For each clue, open the source and check:

1. The event happened **in that year** (not across a span).
2. The text and the context line are accurate and neutral.
3. The clue does not give the year away on its own.
4. The source link works and supports the date.

BCE years use astronomical numbering (one minus the BCE number: 52 BCE is stored as -51).

**Details from memory, not from the year pages. Please check these first:**

- 52 BCE: Avaricum's people being almost all killed (Caesar's own account); the Appian Way murder being in January and leading to riots; Cicero being rattled by the crowd and soldiers, and later writing out the speech; Milo going into exile; the siege lines facing both ways.
- 212 BCE: Tarentum's citadel staying Roman and the city being retaken a few years later; Crassus holding the office for nearly thirty years; the claw lifting ships; Archimedes dying in the sack; the Epang Palace never being finished and the Xiang Yu story.
- 31 BCE: the harpax being fired from a catapult; Antony's ships being bigger; the Judea earthquake details from Josephus and the Dead Sea geology; the Thiruvalluvar calendar counting from this year.

**Weak points:**

- 212 BCE, Technology: Archimedes' machines worked across a siege that ran from 213 to 212 BCE, so the clue is anchored on the capture in 212 BCE. The year page lists it under 212 BCE. Archimedes' own death is only dated to about 212 BCE, so it is not used as a clue.
- 31 BCE, Arts & Culture: Tiruvalluvar's birth date is traditional and disputed. It is the weakest clue here. If you do not want it, the year stays out of the pool.
- 52 BCE, Technology: archaeologists say the lines at Alesia were not continuous.

**Checked and left out.** 105 CE (Cai Lun and paper, and the Danube bridge, both dated to 105 CE on the year page) has no exact-year Society or Arts & Culture clue. 218 BCE (Hannibal and the Alps) has no Arts & Culture clue, and its dates for the crossing and the Trebia are disputed.
